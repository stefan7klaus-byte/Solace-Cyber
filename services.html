A closer look

Practical guidance, matched to your situation.

Every case starts with the information and records you already control. The goal is to understand the situation, identify safe options, and avoid unnecessary risk—not to promise an outcome before the facts are known.

⌕

Initial situation review

Describe the wallet or platform, when access was lost, the devices or backups you still have, and the steps already attempted. Keep the description general at first and leave secrets out.

▤

Backup and file planning

Review what types of backups may be relevant, such as an old computer, external drive, wallet export, or written notes. Preserve originals and make working copies before attempting changes wherever practical.

⌘

Password-clue organization

Organize non-sensitive recollections about password patterns, approximate dates, software versions, and previous devices. Avoid repeatedly guessing passwords if that could lock an account or alter data.

◇

Recovery phrase context

Discuss whether a phrase is incomplete, out of order, or associated with a particular wallet standard. Never place actual seed words or private keys in a website form, email, or chat.

⛨

Risk and provider checks

Before committing to any service, ask for written scope, costs, privacy practices, data-handling arrangements, and limitations. Be cautious of guarantees, pressure, and unexplained upfront payments.

✓

Clear next-step guidance

Use the available facts to decide whether to preserve evidence, seek wallet-specific documentation, contact an official platform, or consult an appropriately qualified professional.

What we will never ask you to post here

Do not send your seed phrase, private key, wallet password, one-time code, remote-access code, or recovery-file contents through this website. A safe first conversation can begin with a high-level description.

Blockchain transaction and address research

Public blockchains can reveal transactions, timestamps, balances reported by an explorer, and movement between addresses. This is read-only research: it cannot reverse a transaction, reveal a private key, confirm a real-world identity on its own, or guarantee that assets can be recovered.

What an initial review covers

Understand the facts before authorizing work.

A responsible first step is to describe the issue without revealing secrets. We can discuss the wallet or platform, approximate dates, devices and backups available, what changed, and which steps have already been attempted.

If a case appears suitable for further work, request a written scope that explains proposed tasks, access permissions, fees, privacy and data-retention practices, risks, communication expectations, and limitations before authorizing anything.

Your seed phrase and private keys must remain private.

Never share seed words, private keys, wallet passwords, two-factor authentication codes, recovery codes, or remote-access credentials with us or anyone else through ordinary messages. A legitimate first conversation does not need these secrets. Crypto recovery cannot be guaranteed; some wallets are permanently inaccessible, and transactions generally cannot be reversed by a recovery service.
